A MagSafe badge on a phone case is a purchase decision: someone will try a
charger or a wallet and expect it to snap. Getting that wrong is worse than
leaving the badge off. This is how merchandising at Y2KASE actually files
MagSafe — the same rules the ingest pipeline and the admin review queue use.

It is **our catalog tag**, not Apple certification. Live counts of how much of
the shop currently carries that tag are on
[What's in the catalog](/insights). The shoppable set is
[MagSafe phone cases](/collections/magsafe).

## The rule we will not break

MagSafe is **not** inferred from the word appearing in a title or description.
An earlier version of the pipeline asked a writing model to mention MagSafe in
the title whenever it thought the case was MagSafe, then treated that mention as
a second "signal." One guess was counted twice. The review queue never fired.

The copy model is now forbidden from writing MagSafe. Wording is applied only
after a confirmed decision, from a single function, so the flag and the prose
cannot corroborate each other.

## What counts as evidence

A dedicated photo pass (temperature 0, not the creative copy pass) has to name
what it saw:

- a **magnet ring** moulded into the back or inside of the case
- the **word MagSafe** printed on the case, packaging, or listing art
- a charger, wallet, or stand **snapped on** with no clip or strap

Anything else is treated as no evidence. A boolean "this looks MagSafe" without
an admissible reason is not enough to badge a live product.

## How a product is routed

Every product is routed to one of three states:

1. **Confirmed** — a human said it is MagSafe (listing spec, operator edit), or
   the photo pass saw admissible evidence at **high** confidence. Only then do
   we apply the `magsafe` tag and the MagSafe line in the title and description.
2. **Review** — plausible but unproven. The product is queued for a human and
   the **live copy is left unmarked**. Shoppers do not see a MagSafe claim while
   we are still looking.
3. **None** — not MagSafe. If we had previously claimed it, we strip the tag and
   every MagSafe sentence from the title and description.

The bias is **false negatives**. A missed MagSafe case can still be confirmed
later. A false MagSafe badge is a charger that will not hold.

A provisional flag from the creative pass can send a product to review. It
cannot confirm MagSafe on its own. With no photo verdict, model output stops at
the queue.

## What you see on the product

Confirmed products:

- carry the `magsafe` tag
- appear in [MagSafe phone cases](/collections/magsafe)
- get a title suffix and a description line that they snap to MagSafe chargers
  and accessories

If we later dismiss or veto a product, we remove the tag **and** every MagSafe
claim in the copy — not only the suffix we added. Leaving a mid-sentence
mention would keep advertising a feature we had just decided the case does not
have.

## Chargers, wallets, grips, charms

- **Chargers and wallets.** A tagged case has the ring those accessories use.
  Third-party pull strength still varies. Unmarked cases should not be expected
  to hold a MagSafe charger or wallet.
- **Grips.** Some styles include a MagSafe-compatible pop grip. That is a
  product option, not a reason to tag an unmarked case.
- **Charms.** Charms clip on. They are not MagSafe. See
  [how to style phone charms](/blog/how-to-style-phone-charms).

Fit is still iPhone 13 through iPhone 18, including Pro and Pro Max — pick the
model on the [iPhone cases](/devices/iphone) product page.
